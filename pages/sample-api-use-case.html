Publish a Help Article with an API

A task-based walkthrough: turn a support answer into a published self-service article.

What you’ll build

Your support team repeatedly answers password-reset questions. You want to turn an approved answer into a help article through an integration, then verify that readers can access it.

This walkthrough creates a draft, leaves room for editorial review, publishes it, and checks the final status. It uses the fictional Atlas Knowledge API.

Before you begin

  • A fictional workspace with the existing category cat_getting_started.
  • A token with articles:write, articles:read, and articles:publish scopes.
  • cURL in a Bash-compatible shell, or Postman for adapting the requests.
  • An approved password-reset procedure matching the product you document.

In a real integration, store the token as ATLAS_API_TOKEN through your environment or secret manager. The examples below are illustrative and cannot run against a live service.

1. Create the draft

Send the title, stable slug, existing category ID, and article content to POST /articles. New articles are drafts by default, so they are not visible to readers yet.

curl --request POST 'https://api.atlasknowledge.example/v1/articles' \
  --header "Authorization: Bearer $ATLAS_API_TOKEN" \
  --header 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  --data '{
    "title": "Reset your password",
    "slug": "reset-your-password",
    "category_id": "cat_getting_started",
    "body_markdown": "## Reset your password\n1. Select **Forgot password** on the sign-in page.\n2. Enter your email address.\n3. Open the reset link sent to your inbox.",
    "locale": "en"
  }'

Expect 201 Created. Save the returned article ID; subsequent requests use it. This example returns art_001, with status: draft and public_url: null.

Use the ID returned by your own request in a real integration. Do not hard-code this example ID.

2. Review before publishing

Retrieve the draft and review its title, category, locale, and steps.

curl 'https://api.atlasknowledge.example/v1/articles/art_001' \
  --header "Authorization: Bearer $ATLAS_API_TOKEN"

Expect 200 OK and status: draft. Confirm that button labels match the UI, the email delivery step is accurate, and the article contains no internal notes or personal data. Ask a product SME to verify the procedure.

If the draft needs changes, edit it in the fictional workspace UI and retrieve it again. This sample does not define an update endpoint.

3. Publish the approved article

After review, call the publish endpoint with the saved article ID. No body is required.

curl --request POST 'https://api.atlasknowledge.example/v1/articles/art_001/publish' \
  --header "Authorization: Bearer $ATLAS_API_TOKEN"

Expect 200 OK. Confirm status: published and a non-null public_url, such as https://help.atlasknowledge.example/en/reset-your-password.

4. Verify the reader experience

Retrieve the article again to check its published status. In a real system, open the returned public URL in a signed-out browser and verify that the title, numbered steps, and formatting display correctly.

  • The article appears in the intended category and language.
  • The instructions match the actual password-reset flow.
  • Readers can access it without a workspace editor account.

The reserved example URL in this sample is not a live help page.

Documentation approach

The walkthrough follows a developer’s task, while the separate reference defines the API contract. Draft creation and publication are separate so that editorial review happens before content becomes public. Expected responses and troubleshooting steps help readers recognise success and recover from failures.
